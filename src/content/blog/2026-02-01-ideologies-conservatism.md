If Liberalism asks "How can we be free?", Conservatism asks a different question:

"What is worth preserving?"

Conservatism is less concerned with abstract and more focused on continuity, stability and the accumulated wisdom of tradition. It views society, not as something to be redesigned from scratch, but as something inherited, maintained and cautiously improved.

---

## What is Conservatism?

At its core, Conservatism is an ideology that emphasizes tradition, social order and gradual change.

Rather than trusting reason or theory alone, conservatism values institutions that have evolved over time -- family, religion, community, nation and law. Change is not rejected outright, but approached carefully, with skepticism toward rapid or radical reform.

Conservatism emerged in response to revolutionary movements of the 18th and 19th centuries, particulary the French Revolution. One of its earliest and most influential thinkers was:

- Edmund Burke [1] - Critic of radical revolution and defender of tradition.

Other influential figures include:

- Russell Kirk [2] - Moral tradition and cultural continuity.
- Michael Oakeshott [3] - Skepticism of rationalist social engineering.

---

## Core Characteristics

While conservatism varies widely across cultures, common themes include:

- Respect for tradition and custom.
- Social order and stability.
- Gradual, evolutionary change.
- Skepticism of centralized power and utopian reform.
- Strong institutions (family, religion, nation).

Conservatism often prioritizes duty, responsibility and continuity over individual autonomy alone.

---

## Strengths and Criticism

Common strengths include:

- Social stability and conitnuity.
- Preservation of cultural identity.
- Resistance to reckless or unintended consequences.
- Emphasis on responsibility and restraint.

Common criticisms include:

- Resistance to necessary reform.
- Reinforcement of outdated hierarchies
- Exclusion of marginalized groups.
- Moral rigidity.

As with Liberalism, conservatism's strengths often double as its weaknesses.

---

## Conservatism in Practice:

### United Kingdom [4]

Modern conservatism traces muchs of its intellectual lineage to Britain.

- Strong emphasis on parliamentary tradition.
- Gradual reform rather than revolution.
- Enduring institutions like the monarchy and common law.

### United States [5]

American conservatism blends tradition with constitutionalism.

- Emphasis on limited government and federalism.
- Strong role for religion and civil society.
- Reverence for the Constitution as a stabilizing framework.

### India [6]

Indian conservatism is culturally rooted rather than strictly ideological.

- Emphasis on tradition, religion and social continuity.
- Preservation of cultural identity in a modern democractic framework.
- Tension between reform and heritage.
