For years, I was the "yes" person. I said yes to every request, every demand—thinking that it would make me more loved and appreciated. But over time, this constant need for approval drained me. I began questioning my own worth, asking myself: When will I be enough? It wasn’t until I started setting boundaries that I found freedom.

The turning point came when a friend asked me why I always prioritized others, even when it left me exhausted. I realized I was afraid of letting people down, but in doing so, I was letting myself down. I thought being helpful would make people love me more, but it only made me feel invisible and undervalued.

I decided to take baby steps toward reclaiming my life. I started saying no to things that didn’t serve me. It wasn’t easy, but each small act of self-respect made a big difference. I learned that setting boundaries doesn’t push people away—it teaches them how to treat you.

People-pleasing is a trap, and escaping it means learning to value yourself. If you’re struggling to set boundaries, remember that self-respect comes first. You can’t pour from an empty cup.

Here are a few steps you can take today to stop people-pleasing:

Practice self-compassion—understanding that boundaries protect both you and your relationships.
